## What is an ETF?

An **ETF (Exchange Traded Fund)** is similar to a mutual fund — open ended, holds assets (predominantly stocks or bonds) — but trades on exchange like an equity, providing **intra-day liquidity**. In Europe most ETFs are within the UCITS structure, regulated by the CBI (Ireland) or CSSF (Luxembourg).

Most ETFs track an index (trackers), but **active ETFs are becoming more popular** with significant growth.

## Key Market Trends

Two central themes for ETFs: **evolution** and **growth**.

From single share class passive equity trackers to today's products: active and smart beta strategies, multi-currency, multi-shareclass, hedged/unhedged, accumulating/distributing, including alternative and real assets and potentially tokenised versions.

- By end 2010, European ETF assets reached just $313bn
- **By 2019, ETF assets hit $1tn**
- Since then: **double-digit compound annual growth**
- **ETF assets double every 5 years**
- **JP Morgan forecasts European ETF assets will reach $6tn by end 2030; EY predicts $4.5tn**

## Why Launch an ETF?

Key selling points:
- **Accessibility**: traded digitally, accessible to all investor types
- **Liquidity**: intra-day trading
- **Transparency**: fund holdings published daily
- **Low cost**: generally lower pricing than mutual funds
