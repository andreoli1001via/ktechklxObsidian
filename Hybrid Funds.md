# The Rise of Hybrid Funds: A New Paradigm for Non-Listed Investment Funds and Long-Term Savings

📅 06/16/2026

*By Bertrand Turquier, Group Product Manager PERES at CACEIS*

The emergence of hybrid funds in Europe is reshaping the non-listed investment fund ecosystem. For asset managers, these vehicles address the search for new sources of recurring income. Growing interest from retail investors in non-listed funds presents a major democratisation opportunity. This trend is supported by national and European policymakers. The ELTIF reform, France's Loi Industrie Verte, and the forthcoming European Savings and Investment Union strategy are accelerating this movement.

## Hybrid Funds: Key Distinctive Features

On the asset side, hybrid funds combine traditional investment strategies in non-listed assets with liability management mechanisms and the less complex operating rules of mainstream funds: perpetual life, redemption optionality, straightforward settlement, immediate investment, and authorisation for distribution to retail investors.

They invest predominantly in non-listed assets (private equity, private debt, infrastructure, real estate) through an open-ended evergreen structure. Unlike closed-ended funds (lifecycle of 8–10 years), this structure allows investments to be held longer without liquidation constraints.

On the liability side, hybrid funds are distinguished by:
- Broader accessibility for retail investors with lower minimum entry tickets
- Simplified settlement arrangements
- Fully paid-up units (no capital calls)
- Semi-liquidity with periodic redemptions (bi-monthly or monthly) within predefined limits

## Growth Dynamics in France

French life insurers are playing a central role, constituting the **primary distribution channel** via life insurance policies. These vehicles allow insurers to diversify portfolio allocations and meet growing demand from savers for better performing long-term investment solutions.

### Supportive Policy and Regulatory Framework
- **ELTIF 2.0**: Authorises distribution to retail clients, permits redemptions during fund life, eases liquidity and diversification rules
- **France's Loi Industrie Verte**: Directs regulated savings towards financing European companies, requiring insurers to include minimum proportions of non-listed assets within life insurance contracts
- **Savings and Investment Union (SIU)**: Proposed framework to create a more integrated European market for long-term savings

### Long-Term Market Prospects
- Investment growth in non-listed assets forecast at ~10% per annum until 2030
- Private credit assets in Europe could more than double over next five years
- Driven by regulatory initiatives, retreat of traditional banks, and increased spending on infrastructure and defence
- By 2030, significant redirection of European savings into private markets expected

## Implications for Asset Servicing

The rise of hybrid funds requires custodians and fund administrators to:
- Automate workflows for more frequent NAV calculation cycles
- Handle high volumes of investors and transactions
- Manage multiple distribution channels
- Implement advanced liquidity monitoring mechanisms
- Achieve greater connectivity between mainstream and non-listed universes

## CACEIS' Approach

**In custody/depositary**: Enhanced platforms accommodate all fund structures (including SPVs) and a wide range of listed and non-listed financial assets.

**Additional services**: Middle-office service for private and syndicated loans, international distribution support via Luxembourg branch, and tailored financing solutions to manage asset/liability cash flow mismatches.

## Conclusion

Hybrid funds represent **a structural evolution** for the European non-listed investment fund market. For retail clients, they offer simplified access to assets historically reserved for institutional investors, with attractive return potential and a more transparent investment experience.

By 2030, the expected growth in non-listed asset investment, combined with the central role of life insurance in France, creates a considerable development reservoir for hybrid funds. Asset servicing players must adapt tools, skills and service offerings to position themselves as indispensable partners for this new generation of funds.
